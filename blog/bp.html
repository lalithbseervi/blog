Introduction

In recent years, there have been numerous reports of cybertheft and cybercrime that has left individuals and families devastated. While these events were unfortunate, many of them could be prevented given that there was enough public awareness. The world of cybersecurity is changing rapidly, and hence, it is important to be aware of the different tricks that can be used against us. In this article, we will take a brief look at some basis preventative measures to keep ourselves safe over the internet.

Fundamental Steps:

  1. Secure your accounts with passwords that are atleast 8 characters long, and are alphanumeric, i.e, alongside characters, use numbers; special characters such as !, @, #, $, %, ^, &, * if possible.
  2. Use 2-Factor Authentication.
  3. Do not open messages from unknown persons or click unknown sites.
  4. Do not open/download '.apk' files or links that start with 'http://'. All secure apps are available through Google Play Store/App Store, and all secure links begin with 'https://'.
  5. Ignore SMS messages that contain:
    1. Information about winning a lottery
    2. About 'exclusive offers'
  6. Do not give your phone to anyone. If someone asks for your phone to call someone, ask them for the number and put the phone on speaker, while ensuring that you have the phone.

Threat-specific prevention:

  1. Stalking
    • Use private social media accounts. Public social media accounts are best kept for businesses/organizations/companies.
    • Do not accept friend/follow requests from unknown persons.
    • Do not upload public stories/posts/vlogs while you are travelling in unknown/unfamiliar territory. Leave atleast a few hours gap between what you are uploading and your current location as these public stories can be used to stalk.

  2. SIM Cloning
    allows bad actors to have an exact replica of your SIM card, which gives them access to sensitive information such as contacts, call records, OTPs, etc. To prevent this:
    • Don't deposit your phone anywhere or give it to strangers.
    • If depositing phone is unavoidable, find your carrier's default SIM lock (it usually is 1234), and change it to some other PIN that you can remember. Now, when someone tries to access your SIM using their device, they must first enter this PIN. Note that this will occur even when you restart your own device.

  3. Call forwarding
    Call forwarding can allow bad actors to access OTPs and other sensitive data. To protect against it:
    • Check for call forwarding if you gave your phone to someone or deposited it somewhere.
    • Do not give your phone to anyone just because they need to contact urgently. Dial the number yourself, put the phone on speaker, while ensuring that you don't give the phone to any stranger. They may use such opportunities to enable call forwarding.

  4. Phishing
    A phishing attempt usually speaks of the following:
  • Urgency: "We noticed some suspicious activity or login attempts"
  • there's a problem with your account/payment information
  • confirmation of personal or financial information
  • send a link to you for making a payment
  • Temptation: you have been 'specially selected' or are 'eligible' for refund/special job offer, coupon offers, hefty discounts, free services/products, etc.

These also carry some traits such as:

  • Bad Grammar
  • Incorrect domains/information
  • First time senders

What to do?

  • Do not give any personal information to someone if you weren't the one to start the communication.
  • Do not click links or respond to emails from legitimate-looking comapnies. Verify the communication by contacting the company, and proceed only if the company verifies the communication was sent by them.
  • There is no such 50% sale expiring in 13 minutes, cash prize of Rs. 50,000. It is an attempt to compromise your logical thinking.
